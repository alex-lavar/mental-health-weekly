This Week's Highlights

Massive Study of 463,000 Teens Links Cannabis Use to Double the Risk of Serious Mental Illness

A landmark cohort study found that adolescents who use cannabis face substantially elevated risk of psychotic and bipolar disorders, with cannabis exposure often preceding diagnosis by nearly two years. Researchers say the magnitude and temporal ordering of the association strengthen the case for a causal relationship, adding urgency to youth prevention efforts.

FDA Accelerates Action on Serious Mental Illness Treatments Following White House Executive Order

Following President Trump's April 2026 executive order, the FDA is issuing National Priority Vouchers for breakthrough-designated treatments and expanding Right to Try pathways—including for ibogaine compounds and other psychedelic therapies. The move could put novel treatments in patients' hands years ahead of traditional approval timelines, though clinical safety oversight questions remain under discussion.

APA July Journals: Digital Therapeutics, Redlining's Mental Health Legacy, and Genetic Overlap in Psychiatric Disorders

The July 2026 American Journal of Psychiatry and companion journals publish new research on FDA and CMS frameworks for digital therapeutics, the lasting mental health impact of historical redlining, and genetic overlap between severe mental disorders and metabolic markers. A guest podcast with researcher Dilip Jeste focuses on social determinants as a frontier for biopsychosocial care.

More Than a Third of Psychologists Report Patients Using AI as a Mental Health Supplement

A new APA survey finds 34%+ of psychologists have patients who turn to AI chatbots as an adjunct between therapy sessions. The report surfaces concerns about safety guardrails, clinical liability, and the risk of over-reliance on AI for crisis support—while acknowledging AI's potential role in expanding access for underserved populations.

UK Opens Consultation on Once-in-a-Generation Cross-Government Mental Health Strategy

The UK government launched a comprehensive national mental health strategy with a public call for evidence open until July 10, 2026, framing it as the most significant overhaul of mental health policy in a generation. The strategy targets a fundamental shift toward earlier intervention and prevention, moving care upstream from crisis services.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Childhood ADHD Medication Does Not Raise Psychosis Risk, Two New JAMA Psychiatry Studies Find

Two independent studies published in JAMA Psychiatry found no elevated risk of adult psychosis or worsened PTSD symptoms in children treated with methylphenidate compared to untreated peers. The findings directly refute a longstanding clinical concern that has led some providers to avoid or delay stimulant prescriptions, particularly in youth with trauma histories.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) Patients Report Growing Pharmacy Stock-Outs of Vraylar Despite No Formal FDA Shortage Declaration

Demand for Vraylar (cariprazine) has grown substantially since its 2022 FDA approval for adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder, but stocking hasn't kept pace—with many patients reporting long wait times and local unavailability. No generic alternative exists before patent expiry around 2029, leaving patients with limited substitution options while AbbVie works to stabilize distribution.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) UK Issues Tier 2 Supply Alert for Venlafaxine 37.5 mg Modified-Release Tablets Through Mid-July

On June 13, 2026, the UK Department of Health and Social Care issued a Tier 2 medicine supply notification for venlafaxine 37.5 mg modified-release tablets, which are expected to remain out of stock through mid-July. Modified-release capsules, immediate-release tablets, and oral solutions are available as alternatives, and pharmacies have been notified through NHS systems.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

MDMA and Psilocybin Therapy Rules Relaxed — Compass Eyes FDA Filing by End of 2026

Australia has updated its psychedelic-assisted therapy protocols, allowing psychiatrists to leave the session room after administration with a qualified therapist maintaining presence throughout—a practical shift aimed at scaling the model. In parallel, Compass Pathways has signaled plans to file for FDA approval of psilocybin treatment for depression by the end of 2026, marking what could be a pivotal regulatory year for the field.

Europe's First Physician-Led Ibogaine Clinic Opens in Malta With Integrated Somatic Protocols

Ambio Life Sciences launched Europe's first physician-led ibogaine program in Malta in January 2026, offering five-day residential programs with 24/7 cardiac monitoring and integrated somatic therapies—breathwork, massage, vagus nerve stimulation, and cold-plunge therapy. The opening coincides with a US executive order signed in April 2026 fast-tracking ibogaine's transition from experimental underground use toward federal therapeutic recognition.

Global Wellness Institute Tracks 2026 as a Pivotal Year for Psychedelic Healing Integration

The Global Wellness Institute's 2026 psychedelics trend report finds that psilocybin and MDMA are transitioning from clinical trials into early regulatory frameworks across multiple jurisdictions, while somatic-integration practices like breathwork are increasingly paired with psychedelic sessions to extend and consolidate therapeutic gains. Researchers note that community-based and indigenous ceremonial contexts are influencing clinical protocol design.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

SSWR 2026: Centering Indigenous and Ancestral Healing in Mental Health — A Decolonizing Framework

Presented at the Society for Social Work and Research 30th Annual Conference, this paper examines how incorporating Indigenous epistemologies—storytelling, ritual, and plant-based ceremony—into clinical practice challenges Western frameworks that pathologize culturally normative behaviors and community-based expressions of distress. The work calls for collective-wellbeing models over individual pathology and highlights systemic barriers including licensing constraints and lack of funding for culturally specific services.

New APA Publication: Decolonizing Mental Health Practice Through Traditional Healing Approaches

A newly indexed APA chapter explores how integrating traditional ceremonies—including talking feather circles, pipe ceremony, naming ceremony, blessing-way, and Native American Church practices—into therapeutic frameworks can address historical trauma and intergenerational wounds in Indigenous communities. The authors identify institutional resistance and licensing structures as the primary barriers preventing culturally grounded care from scaling within mainstream mental health systems.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Maine Breaks Ground on 16-Bed Youth Mental Health Facility in Saco, Serving Under-21s Around the Clock

Governor Mills celebrated the groundbreaking of a new residential treatment facility in Saco providing 24/7 intensive psychiatric care for Maine youth under 21 with acute behavioral health needs. The 16-bed facility—staffed by psychiatrists and behavioral health specialists—fills a critical statewide gap between outpatient care and adult inpatient hospitalization.

California Announces New Wave of Behavioral Health Infrastructure Projects Statewide

Governor Newsom unveiled a new round of Proposition 1-funded behavioral health projects aimed at expanding crisis services, residential treatment beds, and recovery programs in communities with historically limited mental health infrastructure. The investments are part of California's broader effort to reduce reliance on emergency departments and jails as default mental health responders.

New Jersey Opens State's First Crisis Receiving Stabilization Center in Newark

New Jersey health officials and Rutgers University opened the state's first crisis receiving stabilization center in Newark, offering adults in acute mental health or substance use crisis up to 24 hours of medication, peer support, and rest without requiring an emergency room visit. The model is designed to divert people from hospitals and law enforcement, connecting them to community-based follow-up care instead.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Centanafadine: New Non-Scheduled ADHD Drug Faces FDA Decision July 24, 2026

Centanafadine, a triple reuptake inhibitor targeting dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin simultaneously, has a PDUFA action date of July 24, 2026. If approved, it would become the first non-scheduled ADHD medication in years, potentially reducing prescribing barriers—but independent safety and efficacy review is still pending, and real-world performance relative to established stimulants remains unknown.

Elunetirom Shows Positive Phase 2 Data for Bipolar Depression, But Phase 3 Confirmation Awaited

Autobahn Therapeutics announced that elunetirom—a thyroid hormone receptor beta agonist—met its primary and all key secondary endpoints in the Phase 2 AMPLIFY-BD trial across bipolar I and II depression, with a favorable safety profile and no thyroid disruption signals. The results are early-stage and require Phase 3 replication before clinical implications can be confidently drawn, but the novel mechanism has generated interest as a non-dopaminergic option for bipolar depression.

VA Launches First Federally Funded Psychedelic Therapy Studies Since the 1960s

The Department of Veterans Affairs is now actively running approximately a dozen clinical trials into MDMA, psilocybin, and related substances, with the VA Secretary personally visiting an MDMA trial site at a VA health center in the Bronx. Brown University and Yale researchers are evaluating MDMA-assisted therapy for veterans with co-occurring PTSD and alcohol use disorder—outcomes are pending, and the program's scope could expand or contract based on early results and political winds.
