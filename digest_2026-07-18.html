This Week's Highlights

FDA Finalizes Psychedelic Trial Guidance, Formally Solving the Unblinding Problem That Blocked MDMA Therapy

On July 14, the FDA published its final guidance on psychedelic drug clinical investigations in the Federal Register — the most consequential regulatory step in psychedelic medicine since MDMA therapy's 2024 rejection. The guidance formally requires active placebos, blinding questionnaires, and expectancy controls to address "functional unblinding," the core flaw where patients easily guess their treatment assignment, inflating subjective outcomes. A public hearing on psychedelic therapeutic use is also scheduled for September 14, signaling the agency is now actively building the institutional infrastructure for potential approvals.

Trump Administration Formally Backs Ibogaine, Psilocybin, and Methylone — Directs HHS to Prioritize Rescheduling

The White House formally endorsed ibogaine, psilocybin, and methylone as investigational therapies via executive order, directing HHS to expedite rescheduling and expand access under the "Right to Try" law for treatment-resistant conditions. The move translates into real regulatory momentum: it supplements the FDA's April fast-track vouchers for Usona Institute (psilocybin/MDD) and Transcend Therapeutics (methylone/PTSD), and removes a key legal impediment to domestic ibogaine research. Bipartisan support for psychedelic medicine is now operating at the highest levels of the federal government.

Australian RCT: Methylphenidate and Dextroamphetamine Equally Effective for ADHD — But Differ Sharply on Weight

Published July 17 in the Journal of Paediatrics and Child Health, one of the first long-duration head-to-head randomized trials comparing the two most-prescribed ADHD medications found both produce equivalent symptom improvement over 12 months. The meaningful difference: children on dextroamphetamine lost an average 1.4 kg in the first three months versus 300 grams on methylphenidate. For clinicians treating children with lower baseline weight or nutritional concerns, the finding is an evidence-based reason to reach for methylphenidate first.

Vagus Nerve Stimulation Delivers Durable Relief in Treatment-Resistant Depression for 2+ Years

New research confirms vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) helps patients with long-standing, medication-resistant major depressive disorder achieve meaningful improvement — and sustain it for at least two years. With roughly one-third of MDD patients failing to respond adequately to antidepressants, VNS is gaining traction as a viable neuromodulation option for this underserved population, and the new durability data strengthens the case for broader insurance coverage.

Global Genetics Study Reveals Shared Biological Architecture Across 14 Mental Health Disorders

A major international genetics study identified deep genetic overlap across 14 psychiatric conditions — including depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, and ADHD — explaining much of why these disorders so frequently co-occur in the same individuals. The findings point toward shared biological pathways that may eventually yield treatments effective across diagnostic categories, rather than the current disorder-by-disorder pharmacological approach.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate 12-Month RCT: Methylphenidate Causes Significantly Less Weight Loss Than Dextroamphetamine in Children With ADHD

In the most detailed direct comparison of these two medications to date, Australian researchers found methylphenidate and dextroamphetamine produce statistically equivalent ADHD symptom control over a year, but methylphenidate is associated with 78% less weight loss in the first three months (300g vs. 1.4 kg). No significant height difference was found between groups. The study, published July 17, is expected to inform prescribing guidelines for pediatric ADHD cases where growth is a clinical concern.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) Cariprazine Enters Phase 4 Trial for Social Anxiety Disorder, Expanding Beyond Current Indications

Despite a recent industry-sponsored trial failing to separate cariprazine from placebo as a depression augmentation agent, the drug is now advancing into a Phase 4 trial targeting social anxiety disorder — a condition affecting roughly 12% of the U.S. population with limited approved treatment options. The trial aims to determine whether cariprazine's dopamine D3/D2 partial agonism translates to reduced social anxiety, which is biologically distinct from its current MDD indication. Results are anticipated in late 2026.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Meta-Analysis of 82 Trials Quantifies Venlafaxine's Adverse Event Profile — Nausea Affects ~30% of Patients

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 82 randomized clinical trials found venlafaxine is associated with significantly elevated rates of nausea (~30%), dry mouth, constipation, and sexual dysfunction relative to placebo in adults with major depressive disorder. No new approvals or contraindications emerged this week, but the paper's comprehensive adverse event quantification gives clinicians and patients clearer expectations to prepare for — and reinforces guidance to titrate slowly at initiation.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

FDA's Final Psychedelic Guidance Creates a Clearer Regulatory Path for Psilocybin, LSD, MDMA, and Related Compounds

The FDA's finalized guidance published July 14 addresses not only blinding methodology but also nonclinical and clinical safety requirements for the full range of 5-HT2A serotonin receptor agonists — including a new requirement that sponsors developing compounds with 5-HT2B agonist activity conduct echocardiographic assessments to rule out cardiac valvulopathy risk. Compass Pathways and Usona Institute have both indicated their programs are designed to meet the new standards, keeping late-2026 or early-2027 approvals on the table.

Psilocybin Approval Could Come Within Months as FDA Commissioner Sets Late 2026 Target

FDA Commissioner Marty Makary stated publicly that he expects an approval decision by late summer or fall 2026 for at least one of the three priority psychedelic compounds currently under expedited review. Compass Pathways' COMP360 — a synthetic psilocybin formulation studied in treatment-resistant depression across Phase 2 and Phase 3 trials — is the closest to completion. If approved, it would become the first classic psychedelic therapy in U.S. regulatory history.

Ibogaine Derivative Noribogaine Clears First U.S. Clinical Trial Authorization for Alcohol Use Disorder

Miami-based DemeRx NB received the first U.S. clinical trial authorization for noribogaine hydrochloride — a metabolite of ibogaine designed to preserve therapeutic benefit while reducing the cardiac risks (QT-interval prolongation) associated with ibogaine itself. The authorization targets alcohol use disorder, one of the conditions where ibogaine has shown the most promising early data. This is a landmark step toward domestically-approved ibogaine-class therapy, as ibogaine remains Schedule I in the U.S.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

Multi-Country Study: "Two-Eyed Seeing" Models Combining Indigenous and Western Healing Outperform Western-Only Approaches

A newly indexed PubMed study compared how traditional healing frameworks are being integrated into formal mental health practice across Canada, China, Singapore, and the United States, consistently finding that Two-Eyed Seeing models — pairing Western clinical diagnostics with Indigenous wisdom, community ceremony, and land-based practices — produced better outcomes for Indigenous patients than Western-only approaches. The paper argues that these frameworks must be embedded in professional licensing standards and healthcare policy, not treated as optional cultural supplements.

Land-Based Healing Programs Document Measurable Anxiety Reduction and Restored Community Resilience

New 2026 data from land-based healing programs — in which Indigenous participants reconnect with traditional territories through ceremony, seasonal harvesting, and community practice — document measurable reductions in anxiety scores and enhanced social cohesion. Researchers and participants alike note that these gains extend beyond the individual: the healing is explicitly collective, addressing intergenerational trauma in ways that the individual-session model of Western therapy cannot reach. Programs are now being studied for potential integration into national Indigenous health strategies in Canada and New Zealand.

New Research Calls for Integrating Non-Western Perspectives Into Core Mental Health Intervention Frameworks

Published in Culture & Psychology in 2026, a new paper by Misra et al. argues that mainstream counselling and psychotherapy must systematically integrate non-Western, including Indigenous, epistemological frameworks — not as adjuncts, but as co-equal foundations of therapeutic practice. The authors review healing traditions from across Asia, Africa, and the Americas, documenting how community-embedded, relational, and cosmologically grounded care systems address dimensions of human suffering that Western diagnostic categories fail to capture.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Penn State Health Opens 53-Bed Inpatient Psychiatric Unit at Holy Spirit Medical Center in Pennsylvania

Penn State Health officials and Pennsylvania lawmakers gathered July 8 to mark the opening of a new 53-bed inpatient behavioral health unit at Holy Spirit Medical Center in Camp Hill, PA — a major expansion of adult psychiatric capacity in Central Pennsylvania. Administrators described the region's psychiatric bed shortage as chronic and worsening, with emergency departments frequently boarding patients awaiting psychiatric placement for days.

Kentucky's First Regional Mental Health Crisis Intake Center Advances Toward Opening in Bowling Green

The Anchor Project — a partnership between CAYA and the Barren River Area Development District — will be Kentucky's first regional mental health crisis intake center, offering drug detox, therapy, medication management, and 24/7 crisis stabilization under one roof. Construction is progressing as of July 14, and county health officials are positioning the Bowling Green model as a template for future regional crisis centers across the state.

Maine Breaks Ground on Its First Youth Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility — a Long-Awaited $8 Million Project

Governor Janet Mills celebrated the groundbreaking of Maine's first-ever youth psychiatric residential treatment facility — an $8 million renovation of the Sweetser campus in Saco. The 16-bed facility will provide 24/7 intensive psychiatric care for children and adolescents under 21 with acute behavioral health needs, staffed by psychiatrists, mental health clinicians, and nursing professionals. Maine has long been among the states with the fewest inpatient psychiatric beds per capita for youth.

New Guidance Center for Behavioral Health Under Construction in Monroe, Louisiana

A new Guidance Center for Behavioral Health facility broke ground on Stubbs Avenue in Monroe, Louisiana, expanding mental health and substance use services to one of the state's most underserved regions. Organizers expect the facility to open within nine months, adding critical outpatient and crisis service capacity to a region where access to mental health care has historically required travel of more than an hour.

Unverified / Worth Watching

MM120 — an LSD-Derived Compound — Advances as Possible First Psychedelic Treatment for Generalized Anxiety Disorder

MM120 (lysergide distearate), developed by MindMed, is generating significant clinical attention as a potential psychedelic-based treatment for generalized anxiety disorder — a condition where SSRIs and SNRIs leave large numbers of patients inadequately treated. Phase 2 results showed promising symptom reduction, with Phase 3 trials expected to launch in late 2026. The compound is derived from LSD but formulated to optimize therapeutic dose precision. Independent replication of Phase 2 data and full Phase 3 results are needed before clinical conclusions can be drawn.

Ibogaine's "60–88% PTSD Remission" Claims: Compelling Narrative, but Data Primarily From Commercial Retreat Contexts

Ibogaine retreat operators are widely citing 60–88% PTSD symptom reduction figures that circulate prominently in patient-facing marketing. Most of these figures originate from small, uncontrolled observational studies or single-center retreat reports rather than peer-reviewed randomized trials. The Stanford Veterans study remains among the most credible independent data points, and the new DemeRx noribogaine trial represents a step toward rigorous U.S. evidence. Until randomized controlled data mature, these remission figures should be treated as preliminary and contextual — powerful enough to watch, not yet sufficient to cite as clinical benchmarks.
