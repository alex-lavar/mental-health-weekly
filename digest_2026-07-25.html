This Week's Highlights

A Single Dose of Psilocybin Reshapes the Brain — and the Changes Last for Weeks

A study published this week found that a single psilocybin dose produced measurable changes in brain activity and possible structural changes in the brain that lasted for up to a month. The findings add significant weight to the idea that psilocybin's therapeutic effects aren't simply acute — they may trigger lasting neuroplastic reorganization. Researchers say this could explain why clinical trials report durable antidepressant effects well beyond the day of dosing.

APA July Journals: Digital Therapeutics, Redlining's Mental Health Legacy, and Substance Use Across Identity

The July 2026 issues of American Psychiatric Association journals arrived with a wide-ranging slate of research: the role of FDA and CMS in shaping digital therapeutics in psychiatry, divergent genetic overlap between severe mental disorders and metabolic markers, and the documented impact of historic redlining on present-day mental health outcomes. A separate paper examines substance use pattern differences across the sexual identity spectrum, drawing on U.S. national data.

Lancet Psychiatry: GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Linked to Lower Risk of Worsening Mental Illness in Large National Cohort

A major Swedish national cohort study published in The Lancet Psychiatry found that GLP-1 receptor agonist use — the drug class behind Ozempic and Wegovy — was associated with a significantly lower risk of worsening mental illness in people already diagnosed with depression or anxiety. The finding is notable given the scale of GLP-1 prescribing and the long-standing debate about their psychiatric safety. Researchers caution that conflicting evidence exists and further study is needed before clinical guidelines change.

National Inquiry Launched into Interdisciplinary Mental Health Research in the UK

Durham University's Centre for Medical Humanities is leading a rapid review commissioned by the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), examining how cross-disciplinary collaboration — including lived experience perspectives — can generate new solutions to persistent challenges in mental health care. The inquiry aims to identify barriers to integration across disciplines and propose structural changes to how publicly funded research is organized and evaluated.

Definium Therapeutics' LSD-Derived Drug DT120 Meets Phase 3 Primary and Key Secondary Endpoints for Major Depression

Definium Therapeutics announced positive Phase 3 results for DT120 — a lysergide (LSD-class) compound — in major depressive disorder, with the drug meeting its primary and all key secondary efficacy endpoints. The announcement positions DT120 as one of the first synthetic psychedelic compounds to clear Phase 3 efficacy hurdles, separate from the psilocybin and MDMA tracks that have dominated the field. Full data have not yet been peer-reviewed.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Early Methylphenidate Treatment in Children With ADHD May Guard Against Future Psychiatric Disorders

Research highlighted in ADDitude this week finds that pediatric ADHD treatment with methylphenidate may reduce the risk of adverse outcomes later in life, including a protective effect against the development of adult psychiatric disorders. Crucially, children with ADHD who used methylphenidate were no more likely to be diagnosed with psychosis than unmedicated ADHD patients — addressing one of the most persistent concerns among parents and clinicians. Researchers also noted a significant decline in methylphenidate prescriptions following a PTSD diagnosis, raising questions about under-treatment in a complex comorbid population.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) New Analysis Challenges Cariprazine's Efficacy as an Antidepressant Augmentation Agent

Although Vraylar (cariprazine) holds FDA approval for adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder, a new study reviewed by the Carlat Report casts doubt on its real-world utility in that indication — cariprazine failed to separate from placebo after six weeks at standard doses, with only a non-significant trend at the higher 3 mg dose. The drug's metabolic profile remains a genuine advantage over other atypicals (lower risk of weight gain and blood sugar disruption), but the efficacy question in MDD is now open. Clinicians using it for that indication should monitor outcomes closely.

Venlafaxine Venlafaxine Gains Ground as a Dual-Purpose Option for Neuropathic Pain and Mood Disorders

Emerging clinical evidence positions venlafaxine (Effexor) as a valuable dual-use option for patients dealing with both neuropathic pain and comorbid depression or anxiety — conditions that frequently co-occur. While not a first-line neuropathy treatment, venlafaxine's SNRI mechanism offers meaningful relief for a subset of patients where standard analgesics have failed. The drug's relatively low weight-gain profile compared to tricyclic alternatives also adds to its appeal in this population, particularly for longer-term management.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

Psilocybin Triggers Lasting Structural Brain Changes After a Single Session — New Mechanism Evidence

This week's ScienceDaily report on a new psilocybin study moves the conversation beyond "it helps depression" to how: measurable changes in brain structure and activity persist for up to 30 days after a single dose. The research strengthens the neuroplasticity hypothesis — that psilocybin doesn't just alter mood chemistry in the moment but may literally rewire neural pathways in ways that support lasting recovery. It's one of the most mechanistically detailed psilocybin findings to date.

Compass Pathways Targets Q4 2026 NDA Filing for COMP360 in Treatment-Resistant Depression

The most advanced psilocybin drug program in the world — Compass Pathways' COMP360 for treatment-resistant depression — is now targeting a New Drug Application submission to the FDA in Q4 2026. If accepted, the drug could receive an FDA decision by late 2027, making it the first psilocybin compound to potentially reach clinical approval in the U.S. The timeline is bolstered by the FDA's recently finalized psychedelic trial guidance, which provides sponsors clearer regulatory expectations.

Ibogaine Moves from Underground to Federally Recognized Candidate After Trump Executive Order

In April 2026, President Trump signed the Accelerating Medical Treatments for Serious Mental Illness executive order, which mandated National Priority Vouchers and expanded "Right to Try" pathways — effectively fast-tracking ibogaine from a legally ambiguous, primarily offshore therapy to a federally recognized candidate for treatment-resistant PTSD and addiction. Retreat programs offering ibogaine alongside somatic modalities (breathwork, vagus nerve stimulation, somatic experiencing) are now fielding significantly increased referral interest from U.S. veterans and clinicians emboldened by the policy shift.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

Scoping Review: Decolonizing Mental Health Through Traditional Healing Frameworks in Canada, China, Singapore, and the U.S.

A new peer-reviewed scoping review in PubMed identifies how traditional healing frameworks across four countries — including Indigenous practices in Canada and the U.S. — challenge and complement Western psychiatric models. The review finds that decolonial and liberation psychology approaches, when integrated into care, address social-epistemic injustices and create space for communal, spirit-based, and land-rooted healing that clinical psychology alone cannot replicate. Policy changes to support these integrations in mainstream training pipelines remain a gap the authors explicitly flag.

SSWR 2026 Conference: Centering Indigenous and Ancestral Healing in Modern Mental Health Practice

A paper presented at the Society for Social Work and Research 30th Annual Conference calls for a paradigm shift: moving clinical practice beyond the dominant Eurocentric model by centering Indigenous and ancestral healing traditions as valid, evidence-supported frameworks. The presentation challenges the field to validate knowledge systems grounded in community, ceremony, story, and nature — and to restructure how therapy training programs are designed so they don't inadvertently reproduce colonial hierarchies in care delivery.

Traditional Healers in Nagaland: Indigenous Narratives of Mental Illness and the Case for Integrative Care

A qualitative study published in PMC examines how Naga tribal communities in northeastern India understand and respond to mental illness through ancestral healing narratives. Traditional healers are widely regarded as effective mental health resources, and researchers propose formal integration with modern care systems — provided intellectual property protections and mutual respect are built into any partnership. The study adds to a growing body of evidence that culturally rooted healing approaches deserve inclusion in national mental health architectures, not just ethnographic documentation.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Ohio: Ribbon-Cutting for James Hagen Crisis Care Center, Serving Five-County Region

A ribbon-cutting ceremony was held July 21 for the James Hagen Crisis Care Center in Ross County, Ohio — the culmination of a four-year effort to bring comprehensive crisis care for mental health and substance use to Ross, Pickaway, Pike, Highland, and Fayette Counties. Operated by Coleman Health Services for the Paint Valley ADAMH Board, the facility is expected to open this fall after final occupancy permits are issued. The center represents a regional model for consolidating crisis services in rural areas historically underserved by mental health infrastructure.

Penn State Health Opens 53-Bed Inpatient Behavioral Health Unit at Holy Spirit Medical Center

Penn State Health held an opening celebration on July 8 for a new inpatient behavioral health unit at Holy Spirit Medical Center in Pennsylvania, adding 53 new psychiatric beds spread across two floors of the hospital. The expansion is designed to reduce wait times for inpatient psychiatric admissions in the central Pennsylvania region, where demand for acute behavioral health care has significantly outpaced supply since the COVID-19 pandemic. The unit replaces a prior location and is equipped with updated therapeutic infrastructure.

Maine Breaks Ground on State's First-Ever Youth Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility

Construction began this month on Maine's first dedicated youth psychiatric residential treatment facility — a historic milestone for a state that has long sent adolescents out-of-state for intensive mental health care at significant cost and family disruption. The facility will provide long-term residential psychiatric treatment for children and adolescents with complex mental health needs. State health officials say the opening will reduce reliance on expensive out-of-state placements and keep young people closer to their families and support networks during treatment.

Unverified / Worth Watching

GLP-1 Drugs and Psychiatric Risk: One Study Finds 195% Higher Depression Risk — Watch This Closely

While the Lancet Psychiatry cohort study (above) found GLP-1 drugs associated with better mental health outcomes, at least one competing analysis reports alarming findings in the opposite direction: GLP-1 receptor agonist users showed a 195% higher risk of major depressive disorder, 108% higher anxiety disorder risk, and 106% increased suicidal behavior risk. The FDA has concluded based on available data that GLP-1s do not increase suicidal ideation risk — but the scientific picture remains actively contested. This is a space to watch before GLP-1 prescribing for psychiatric indications becomes mainstream.

Definium Therapeutics DT120 (Lysergide): LSD-Derived Compound Passes Phase 3 — Peer Review Pending

Definium Therapeutics' DT120, a synthesized LSD-class compound, announced Phase 3 success for major depressive disorder — but full data have not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal. If the findings hold up to independent scrutiny, DT120 would become the first LSD-derived compound to clear Phase 3 for a mood disorder and would likely attract significant regulatory and commercial attention. The announcement is promising but should be treated as unconfirmed until data are published and independently replicated.

APA Annual Meeting Floats GLP-1 Drugs as Potential Psychiatric Medications — Field Divided

At its annual meeting in San Francisco, the American Psychiatric Association featured presentations proposing new directions for GLP-1 receptor agonists as psychiatric medications, with some researchers arguing the drugs' effects on dopamine and inflammation could make them useful in mood and addiction disorders. The proposal is speculative — no GLP-1 drug is approved for psychiatric indications — but the topic generated significant floor debate. Given the rapidly accumulating (and contradictory) data on GLP-1s and mental health, formal clinical trials targeting psychiatric endpoints directly seem likely in the near term.
