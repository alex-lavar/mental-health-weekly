This Week's Highlights

Psychiatric Times Focuses September on Depressive Disorders — and the Gaps in Who Gets Studied

Psychiatric Times has designated depressive disorders as its September 2026 theme, spotlighting a critical problem: a new systematic review of 397 randomized controlled trials found persistent under-representation of women, people of color, and vulnerable youth in mental health research. The WHO estimates 5.7% of adults worldwide have depression — but the populations most affected are least studied, calling the generalizability of decades of clinical data into question.

Imaging Study Maps Six Distinct Biological Subtypes of Depression

A large-scale cross-ancestry study published in Molecular Psychiatry used neuroimaging-derived phenotypes across nearly 1.4 million individuals to identify six biologically distinct depression subtypes, each driven by different pathways involving metabolic regulation, neurotransmitter dynamics, and neuroimmune interactions. Researchers say the findings mark a meaningful step toward precision psychiatry — matching treatment to a patient's specific neurological profile rather than symptom checklists alone.

GLP-1 Drugs Linked to Lower Risks of Addiction and Overdose in Major Study

A large observational study published in June and still generating discussion found that GLP-1 receptor agonists (the class including semaglutide/Ozempic) were associated with a 47% reduction in hospitalization for substance use disorders and a 44% lower risk of depression during periods of active use. Researchers caution that causality cannot be confirmed from an observational design, but the breadth of the psychiatric signal — spanning depression, anxiety, addiction, and suicidal behavior — is drawing serious attention from psychiatrists and addiction specialists.

Adults With ADHD Face Sharply Higher Risk of Cardiovascular Events After Starting Antihypertensives

New research reveals that adults with ADHD who begin antihypertensive medications experience significantly higher rates of heart failure hospitalization, stroke, and cardiorenal death compared with counterparts without ADHD — a finding with direct clinical implications for the large number of adults simultaneously managing both conditions. Clinicians are being urged to factor ADHD status into cardiovascular risk conversations, particularly as ADHD diagnoses in adults have continued to rise.

California Announces 15 New Behavioral Health Projects Under $5.8B Reform Push

On August 11, Governor Newsom announced 15 new Proposition 1-funded projects expanding crisis, residential, youth, tribal, and community-based behavioral health capacity across California. The announcement brings the state's cumulative behavioral health investment since 2021 to more than $5.8 billion. Among the new groundbreakings: the Yurok Tribe's Wah-Sekw-Won Treatment Center in Humboldt County, backed by a $26 million award, which will add 53 inpatient residential beds and outpatient services for approximately 1,300 people annually.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Multimodal Brain Imaging Study Confirms Methylphenidate's Cognitive Benefits in Children With ADHD

A new study published in Frontiers in Pharmacology used go/no-go cognitive tasks alongside functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) to objectively measure methylphenidate's effects in children with ADHD. The research found meaningful improvements in inhibitory control and prefrontal cortex activation, adding neuroimaging evidence to the established clinical record. A separate RCT also found that pairing methylphenidate with adaptive digital therapeutics produced better outcomes than medication alone — a finding with practical implications for pediatric treatment plans.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) FDA-Approved as Add-On Antidepressant Therapy — Clinicians Assess Real-World Fit

Cariprazine (Vraylar; AbbVie/Gedeon Richter) received FDA approval as an adjunctive therapy for adults with major depressive disorder who have a partial response to their current antidepressant. A Phase 3 trial showed a statistically and clinically significant improvement in depression scores at six weeks for patients on cariprazine 1.5 mg/day plus antidepressant, versus placebo plus antidepressant. With the approval now in place, prescribers are weighing cariprazine's dopamine-receptor partial agonism profile against other augmentation options like aripiprazole and brexpiprazole.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Venlafaxine Gains Attention as Non-Hormonal Option for Menopausal Depression and Hot Flashes

As interest grows in non-hormonal management of menopause symptoms, venlafaxine (Effexor) is seeing renewed clinical attention. Kaiser Permanente research confirms that venlafaxine XR 75 mg/day produces significant improvements in quality of life for postmenopausal women experiencing hot flashes, and existing evidence supports its efficacy for concurrent depressive symptoms — making it a dual-purpose option for patients who cannot or prefer not to use estrogen therapy. Clinicians note that the SNRI's effect on hot flash frequency is modest but quality-of-life gains are clinically meaningful.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

FDA Schedules September 14 Public Hearing on Therapeutic Use of Psychedelic Drugs

The FDA has announced a hybrid public hearing for September 14, 2026, seeking stakeholder perspectives on the future regulatory framework for psychedelic-assisted therapy — including supervised use models, set-and-setting standards, and therapist training requirements. The hearing comes as Compass Pathways' COMP360 (psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression) moves through rolling NDA review, with a final module submission expected in Q4 2026 and a potential approval decision by early 2027 that would mark the first classic psychedelic to reach the U.S. market.

Peer-Reviewed Trial Investigates Breathwork-Induced Altered States as Therapeutic Pathway

A new exploratory study published in Frontiers in Psychology — the Airways to Alteration (A2A) trial — examined breathwork-induced altered states of consciousness in experienced practitioners, finding that controlled breathing techniques can produce experiences phenomenologically similar to mild psychedelic states. Researchers emphasize that empirical study of breathwork remains limited compared to pharmacological interventions, but the trial is part of a broader 2026 trend of academic institutions taking non-drug somatic and breath-based approaches more seriously as complementary mental health tools.

Executive Order and National Priority Vouchers Accelerate Psychedelic Therapy Pipeline

A Trump executive order issued earlier this year has given additional regulatory momentum to psychedelic drug development. The FDA followed by issuing national priority vouchers to companies studying psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression and major depressive disorder, and methylone for PTSD. Combined with breakthrough therapy designations for MDMA and psilocybin programs, the policy signals a structural shift in how federal regulators are approaching plant- and compound-based psychiatric treatments.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

Multi-Country Study Examines Indigenous and Traditional Healing Frameworks as a Model for Decolonizing Mental Health

A new PubMed-indexed study reviewed traditional healing practices across Canada, China, Singapore, and the United States, finding that Indigenous frameworks — grounded in communal ritual, kinship networks, ancestral connection, and spiritual knowledge — produce mental health benefits not captured by standard psychiatric outcome measures. Authors argue that Western psychiatric models systematically pathologize culturally normative behaviors and call for systemic changes in how care is delivered to Indigenous and non-Western communities globally.

Social Work Research Conference Puts Centering of Ancestral Healing Front and Center

At the Society for Social Work and Research 30th Annual Conference, a featured paper titled "Decolonizing Therapy: Centering Indigenous and Ancestral Healing in Mental Health Practices" drew significant attention. The presentation advocated for integrating storytelling, ceremony, land-based healing, and intergenerational trauma frameworks into clinical training — arguing that the field's reliance on individualist, pathology-first models has failed communities with strong collective and ancestral identities.

Yurok Tribe Breaks Ground on Wah-Sekw-Won Treatment Center in Northern California

The Yurok Tribe and Yurok Health and Human Services broke ground on the Wah-Sekw-Won Treatment Center in Humboldt County — a $26 million tribally-led facility that will integrate Indigenous healing practices alongside clinical services. With 53 inpatient residential beds and outpatient capacity projected to serve approximately 1,300 people per year, the center represents one of the most significant investments in Indigenous-led behavioral health infrastructure in recent California history, funded through Proposition 1's Behavioral Health Continuum Infrastructure Program.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Texas Behavioral Health Center Opens in Dallas — a Major Public Psychiatric Infrastructure Addition

The Texas Health and Human Services Commission and UT Southwestern Medical Center celebrated the opening of the Texas Behavioral Health Center in Dallas on June 16, 2026, in a ribbon-cutting ceremony. The new state-of-the-art facility adds substantial inpatient psychiatric capacity to a region that has historically faced significant shortfalls in public mental health beds, and is designed to serve individuals experiencing acute psychiatric crises who might otherwise be held in emergency departments for extended periods.

Hackensack Meridian Opens $40M Child and Adolescent Psychiatric Facility in New Jersey

Hackensack Meridian Health opened a 43,000-square-foot expansion of its Carrier Clinic campus dedicated specifically to children and adolescents in psychiatric crisis. The $40 million facility — designed to address what the health system calls a "critically needed" gap in youth mental health care in the state — adds specialized inpatient and intensive outpatient capacity at a moment when child and adolescent psychiatric beds remain among the scarcest healthcare resources nationwide.

Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe to Operate New Inpatient Psychiatric Facility in Washington State

A new inpatient psychiatric evaluation and treatment facility planned for Sequim, Washington will be developed and operated by the Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe — marking a notable instance of a tribal nation leading a non-reservation psychiatric facility open to the broader community. The center is expected to add critical access to psychiatric evaluation and stabilization services to a rural region of the Olympic Peninsula that currently lacks local inpatient options.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Oral GLP-1 Drugs May Suppress the Brain's Reward Circuit for Food — and Possibly More

A July 2026 animal study found that newer oral GLP-1 drugs related to semaglutide reduced pleasure-driven eating in mice by quieting a deep brain reward circuit. Researchers are watching closely to see whether the mechanism extends to other reward-seeking behaviors in humans — including alcohol, nicotine, and opioid use. The hypothesis is compelling and consistent with earlier observational data, but direct causal evidence in human psychiatric populations is still absent. This remains an area to watch as clinical trials in addiction populations accelerate. Note: current evidence is observational; psychiatric use of GLP-1 drugs is off-label.

Psilocybin Could Become the First FDA-Approved Classic Psychedelic Therapy Within Months

Compass Pathways has announced six-month durability data for COMP360 in treatment-resistant depression, and its rolling NDA with the FDA is on track for completion in Q4 2026. If approved, psilocybin would become the first classic psychedelic to receive FDA sanction — a milestone that would transform the regulatory landscape for ibogaine, LSD, DMT, and other compounds currently in earlier trial stages. However, DEA rescheduling will still be required before prescriptions can be written, and Compass doesn't anticipate a commercial launch before H1 2027. Unverified pending FDA decision.

Methylone for PTSD: The Lesser-Known Psychedelic Now Holding a Priority Voucher

While MDMA and psilocybin dominate psychedelic therapy headlines, the FDA quietly issued a national priority voucher to a methylone (a synthetic empathogen structurally related to MDMA) program for PTSD treatment earlier this year. Methylone's preclinical profile suggests similar prosocial effects to MDMA but with a potentially cleaner safety profile; however, no Phase 3 human data exists yet. Researchers and advocates are watching to see whether the voucher reflects genuine FDA confidence or is a broader signal of openness to the drug class. Emerging — no Phase 3 data yet.
