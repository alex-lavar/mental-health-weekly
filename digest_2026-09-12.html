This Week's Highlights

FDA Schedules First-Ever Public Hearing on Therapeutic Psychedelic Drugs for September 14

The FDA has set September 14, 2026 as the date for its first public hearing on the therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs, following the release of its first-ever final guidance for companies running clinical trials of psilocybin, LSD, and MDMA. The framework reshapes trial design requirements, REMS protocols, and patient access pathways — and arrives as Compass Pathways' psilocybin therapy is in rolling NDA review with final modules expected in Q4 2026.

Definium Therapeutics' Lysergide Tablet (DT120) Earns FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation for MDD

On September 8, Definium Therapeutics received Breakthrough Therapy designation from the FDA for DT120 — an orally disintegrating lysergide (LSD) tablet — following positive Phase 3 results in major depressive disorder. The designation accelerates development and review, and marks the second psychedelic-derived compound to achieve this status for depression after psilocybin. It is among the most significant regulatory milestones in psychiatric drug development this year.

Eli Lilly Closes AtaiBeckley Acquisition, Bringing Three Psychedelic Pipeline Drugs In-House

Stockholders approved the acquisition on September 8, with the deal closing September 11. The transaction brings BPL-003, VLS-01, and EMP-01 — three investigational psychedelic-derived compounds — under Eli Lilly's development umbrella, signaling a major inflection point for mainstream pharmaceutical investment in psychedelic medicine. Analysts note that Lilly's resources and regulatory experience could materially accelerate timelines for these programs.

Childhood Methylphenidate Treatment May Reduce Long-Term Psychosis Risk, Large Cohort Finds

A large cohort study found that prescribing methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta) to younger children with ADHD may lower their long-term risk of developing psychosis in adulthood. Researchers suggest that early and consistent ADHD pharmacotherapy may confer a neuroprotective effect, though they caution that confounding factors must be ruled out before firm causal claims are made.

APA Monitor: Mental Health Apps Consistently Fail to Outperform Active Treatment Controls

The September 2026 APA Monitor examines a recurring finding in app-based mental health research: commercial mental health apps frequently fail to outperform active treatment controls in rigorous trials. The analysis argues this predictable outcome — consistent with decades of broader psychotherapy research — does not necessarily mean apps are useless, but underscores the gap between marketing claims and clinical evidence that regulators and clinicians must navigate carefully.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Systematic Review on Methylphenidate's Addictive Potential Published September 6

A comprehensive systematic review published September 6 in Pharmaceuticals examined human and preclinical behavioral, clinical, and neurobiological evidence on methylphenidate's addictive properties, covering misuse, abuse, tolerance, craving, and withdrawal. The review concludes that addictive potential exists but is highly context-dependent — lower in clinical ADHD populations and higher in non-prescribed use — offering a nuanced counterweight to both over-alarm and dismissiveness about the drug's risk profile.

Vraylar · Cariprazine New Industry-Sponsored Trial: Cariprazine Fails to Separate From Placebo for Depression at 6 Weeks

Despite the FDA's approval of cariprazine (Vraylar) as adjunctive therapy for major depressive disorder, a newly reported industry-sponsored study found the drug failed to statistically separate from placebo at the six-week primary endpoint. The Carlat Report includes this in a summary of recent psychiatric research disappointments, raising questions about trial design, patient selection, and the widening gap between regulatory thresholds and meaningful clinical outcomes for adjunctive antidepressants.

Venlafaxine · Effexor 20-Year Post-Marketing Safety Analysis of Venlafaxine Published in Pharmacovigilance Study

A new real-world pharmacovigilance study using the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) analyzed two decades of venlafaxine safety data, from Q1 2004 through Q2 2025. The paper identifies signal patterns across age groups and comorbid conditions, providing one of the most comprehensive post-marketing safety portraits of the widely prescribed SNRI to date. Findings are expected to inform updated prescribing guidance and post-discontinuation monitoring recommendations.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

What the FDA's New Psychedelic Trial Framework Actually Means for Clinical Practice

Clinical Trial Vanguard breaks down the FDA's newly finalized guidance for psychedelic drug trials, clarifying what the framework changes in practice: tighter REMS requirements, clearer blinding and placebo standards, updated therapist certification thresholds, and new language around set-and-setting documentation. The piece is a practical read for clinicians and researchers trying to interpret what the regulatory shift means for actual trial enrollment and protocol design.

MDMA-Assisted Therapy Resubmitted to FDA — Without a New Efficacy Trial

After the FDA rejected MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD in August 2024, the manufacturer resubmitted the application in August 2026 without running an additional efficacy trial, instead responding to FDA concerns with reanalysis and supplemental data. The resubmission has drawn divided responses from the research community: some see it as a reasonable scientific defense, while others argue a new trial is the only way to resolve the methodological concerns the FDA originally raised.

Integration Science: The 90-Day Neuroplasticity Window After Ibogaine Treatment

A growing body of clinical literature is coalescing around the concept of a structured 90-day integration window following ibogaine therapy, during which somatic work, breathwork, and vagal exercises appear to anchor neuroplastic changes and reduce relapse rates. Programs in Mexico, Thailand, and Portugal are increasingly formalizing this phase as a core component of treatment — not optional aftercare — with one-to-one somatic sessions and structured rest built into the protocol from the outset.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

Heritage Tourism as Mental Wellness: How Cultural Re-Connection Supports Indigenous Psychological Health

A peer-reviewed study in PMC examines how heritage tourism and participation in traditional cultural practices function as active mental health interventions for Indigenous communities, not passive leisure. Strong cultural affiliation was associated with lower anxiety, greater emotional stability, higher self-esteem, and reduced social alienation — outcomes researchers attribute to the sense of symbolic meaning, continuity, and community that cultural practices provide in the context of grief, trauma, and social disruption.

ATTC Network Hosts "Sacred Knowledge, Modern Healing" — Indigenous Wellness Training for Clinicians

The Addiction Technology Transfer Center (ATTC) Network is hosting a training series introducing clinical professionals to Indigenous wellness frameworks, including smudging and purification ceremonies, elder-guided mentorship, storytelling, and land-based activities as therapeutic modalities. The program emphasizes that these practices are not adjuncts to Western care but complete systems of healing that address body, mind, spirit, and relational health simultaneously — and that clinicians integrating them need cultural guidance, not just technique instruction.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

New 10-Bed Mental Health Home Opens in Southeast Portland to Ease State Hospital Backlog

A new residential mental health facility, Mountain View, opened this week in Southeast Portland with 10 beds designed specifically to reduce the backlog of patients awaiting transfer from Oregon State Hospital. The facility targets individuals who are clinically ready to transition from inpatient hospitalization but cannot safely discharge to community settings without a supported step-down environment — a gap that has contributed to prolonged state hospital stays across Oregon.

California Announces 15 New Behavioral Health Projects Under Proposition 1 Reform

Governor Newsom's office announced 15 new behavioral health infrastructure projects across California funded by Proposition 1, with an emphasis on rural and underserved regions. The projects include new outpatient slots, crisis stabilization units, and residential beds, projected to serve over 9,400 individuals annually once fully operational. The announcement represents the latest tranche of the voter-approved $6.4 billion behavioral health overhaul that restructured California's mental health funding model in 2024.

Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe to Open Tribal-Operated Psychiatric Facility in Sequim, Washington

A new inpatient psychiatric evaluation and treatment facility is in development in Sequim, Washington, developed and operated by the Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe — a relatively rare instance of a tribal nation building and controlling its own psychiatric infrastructure. The facility is designed to provide culturally responsive care to tribal members and area residents, and is expected to open in 2026. Tribal healthcare leaders describe it as an act of sovereignty as much as a healthcare investment.

Unverified / Worth Watching

GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Showing Early Signal for Addiction and Depression — Causality Unconfirmed

Multiple observational studies are now associating GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide) with reduced hospitalization for substance use disorders and lower rates of depression during active use, with some showing effect sizes that rival or exceed standard antidepressants. Researchers and clinicians are increasingly taking the psychiatric signal seriously, but the evidence base remains observational — confounding by indication and health behavior changes make it impossible to attribute the psychiatric effects to the drug mechanism alone at this stage.

Gut-Brain Axis and Mitochondrial Function Emerge as Converging Mental Health Research Targets

Two threads of emerging research are drawing increased interest from psychiatric researchers: the microbiome-gut-brain axis as a modifiable factor in mood and anxiety disorders, and the role of mitochondrial function in neurological health. Neither area has produced clinical interventions ready for practice, but preliminary findings are robust enough that major research centers are establishing dedicated programs. The Mind Remake Project's 2026 roundup flags both as areas to track, with the caveat that mechanistic plausibility often outpaces therapeutic reality in early-stage neuroscience.
