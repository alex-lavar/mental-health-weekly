This Week's Highlights

Compass Pathways' Psilocybin Drug Hits Primary Endpoint in Second Phase 3 Trial for Treatment-Resistant Depression

COMP360 (25 mg psilocybin) achieved highly statistically significant reductions in MADRS depression scores versus placebo in the COMP006 trial (p<0.001, –3.8 points), replicating the earlier COMP005 result. With two successful Phase 3 trials now in hand and FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation, Compass is targeting a New Drug Application submission in Q4 2026 — a potential turning point for the roughly 100 million people worldwide with treatment-resistant depression.

FDA Approves Centanafadine (SIMTRIYO) for ADHD; New Data Show Benefit for Comorbid Anxiety

Otsuka's centanafadine (SIMTRIYO) cleared FDA review for ADHD in adults and children aged 6 and older — the first new non-stimulant ADHD mechanism in years. Simultaneously released Phase 3b data show it also significantly reduced generalized and social anxiety symptoms in adults with comorbid ADHD (HAM-A reduction vs. placebo, p<0.05 at week 8), a population historically underserved because stimulants can worsen anxiety.

FDA Finalizes Psychedelic Drug Trial Guidance, Codifying Rules on Blinding and Expectancy Controls

The FDA's final guidance on psychedelic clinical investigations formally answers the "functional unblinding" problem that sank Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA-PTSD application in 2024: sponsors must now include active placebos, blinding questionnaires, and expectancy controls. A public hearing on psychedelic therapeutic use is scheduled for September 14, 2026, signaling sustained regulatory engagement with the field.

APA July 2026 Journals Feature Genetics of Severe Mental Disorders, Digital Therapeutics, and Social Determinants

The American Psychiatric Association's July journal packages cover genetic overlap between severe psychiatric conditions and metabolic markers, evidence-based use of prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs), and a framework for integrating social determinants of health into psychiatric care. Taken together, they reflect psychiatry's broadening from symptom management to whole-person and population-level intervention.

APA Report: More Than a Third of Psychologists Say Patients Now Use AI as a Supplemental Mental Health Provider

A new American Psychological Association survey finds 36% of practicing psychologists have clients who use AI chatbots or large language models as an adjunct to — or replacement for — formal therapy. The report raises questions about safety, therapeutic alliance, and equitable access, while noting that AI tools may be filling genuine gaps in care for people who cannot access or afford a human therapist.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Childhood Methylphenidate Use May Lower Long-Term Risk of Psychosis, Large Study Finds

A large epidemiological study found that children with ADHD prescribed methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta) had meaningfully reduced rates of later psychotic conditions — including bipolar disorder and schizophrenia — compared to untreated peers with ADHD. Researchers note the finding runs counter to earlier concerns that stimulant-class drugs could trigger psychosis, though they caution the mechanism is still unclear and more controlled research is needed.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) FDA Expands Cariprazine (Vraylar) Approval Into Pediatric Bipolar and Schizophrenia

A supplemental FDA approval now extends Vraylar (cariprazine) to pediatric patients with bipolar I disorder and schizophrenia, adding to its existing adult indications for bipolar depression, mania, schizophrenia, and MDD adjunct therapy. Cariprazine's partial agonism at D3/D2 receptors distinguishes it mechanistically from older antipsychotics and has been associated with lower weight-gain risk — an important consideration in pediatric populations.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Systematic Review: Venlafaxine Lowers Depression Scores but Adverse-Event Profile Warrants Careful Monitoring

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials (PMC11561525) confirms venlafaxine produces statistically significant reductions in depression rating scales versus placebo, while underscoring a notable adverse-event burden — including nausea, hypertension, and discontinuation syndrome. The authors call for clearer clinician guidance on tapering protocols and blood-pressure monitoring, particularly in patients with cardiovascular risk factors.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

Psilocybin's Second Phase 3 Win Clears the Bar for FDA Filing — What Comes Next

With COMP360 now posting back-to-back Phase 3 successes for treatment-resistant depression, analysts say a New Drug Application could reach the FDA as early as Q4 2026. If approved, psilocybin-assisted therapy would require a novel delivery infrastructure — trained therapist pairs, certified session rooms, and integration support — prompting healthcare systems to begin planning now.

ScienceDirect Review: Reconsidering Ibogaine for Severe Mental Illness and Substance Use Disorders

A new review in ScienceDirect examines ibogaine's multi-receptor mechanism and its potential beyond opioid withdrawal — including applications in treatment-resistant depression, PTSD, and chronic pain. The authors acknowledge ibogaine's cardiac risk profile and the current absence of FDA approval, but argue that the April 2026 executive order directing expedited pathways for serious mental illness treatments may open the door for controlled U.S. trials.

AI-Driven Proactive Music Therapy: Emerging Research on Anticipatory Emotional Regulation

A Frontiers in Psychology paper introduces the concept of AI-enabled proactive music therapy — systems that detect early markers of emotional dysregulation and deliver adaptive musical interventions before distress escalates. Though preliminary and lacking clinical-trial data, the model draws on established somatic and nervous-system regulation theory, situating it at the intersection of digital therapeutics and mind-body practice.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

SSWR 2026: Decolonizing Therapy — Centering Indigenous and Ancestral Healing in Mental Health Practices

Presented at the Society for Social Work and Research's 30th Annual Conference, this paper calls for policy changes requiring integration of decolonized healing methods into mainstream therapy training and licensure. It centers Indigenous knowledge systems — land-based practices, ceremony, ancestral lineage, and community accountability — as legitimate clinical frameworks, not supplementary add-ons, and challenges the primacy of Western diagnostic categories.

New PMC Study: Indigenous Narratives of Mental Illness Among Naga Tribes Reveal Alternative Explanatory Models

Published in a peer-reviewed journal and indexed on PubMed, this qualitative study documents how Naga tribal communities in northeast India conceptualize mental distress — through spiritual disruption, social disconnection, and ancestral transgression rather than biomedical categories. The findings contribute to a growing evidence base arguing that cultural explanatory models must inform assessment and treatment for Indigenous and diaspora populations, rather than being filtered out as "unscientific."

NATIVation Counseling: Reclaiming Identity, Ceremony, and Healing Through Decolonizing Therapy

NATIVation Counseling, an Indigenous-led practice, outlines its decolonizing therapy model — one that positions prayer, somatic ceremony, and community ritual as primary clinical tools rather than contraindications. The approach challenges the systematic exclusion of spirituality from licensed mental health care, citing evidence that Indigenous communities show stronger recovery outcomes when healing integrates ancestral practices with — or instead of — conventional talk therapy.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Greenbrook Opens National Center of Excellence for Advanced Interventional Psychiatry in Rockville, MD (July 28)

Neuronetics' Greenbrook Mental Wellness Centers debuted a nearly 7,000-square-foot flagship facility in Rockville, Maryland — its largest center to date — dedicated to TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation), esketamine (Spravato), and emerging interventional treatments. The National Center of Excellence designation signals a push to centralize expertise in neuromodulation and treatment-resistant mood disorder care as the interventional psychiatry field matures.

Carrier Clinic Debuts $40M Youth Mental Health Expansion, Lowering Treatment Age to 7

New Jersey's Carrier Clinic opened a new 43,000-square-foot, 52-bed expansion in June 2026, expanding inpatient psychiatric capacity and lowering its minimum treatment age from 12 to 7 years old. The $40 million investment reflects the mounting demand for child and adolescent inpatient psychiatric beds — a sector that has seen years of supply shortages amid rising pediatric mental health crisis rates.

Chicago Launches $16.2M Healthy Chicago Mental Health Collective with Street Psychiatry Pilot

The Chicago Department of Public Health mobilized its network of Mental Health Centers alongside 53 new partner organizations under a $16.2M investment, adding a street psychiatry pilot that brings licensed clinicians directly to unhoused individuals and those in crisis. The initiative explicitly frames equitable access to care — rather than emergency response — as the primary intervention strategy for Chicago's most vulnerable residents.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Frontiers Scoping Review: AI in Mental Health — Promise Is Real, but Evidence Base Remains Thin

A large scoping review in Frontiers in Psychiatry synthesizes dozens of reviews on AI applications in mental health — diagnosis, risk prediction, therapy delivery — and finds consistent theoretical promise but weak clinical-trial validation. Most studies involve small samples, short follow-up periods, and lack active-comparator controls. Worth watching as a field-level signal: AI mental health tools are proliferating faster than evidence can evaluate them.

Centanafadine's Anxiety Signal: Real Benefit or Secondary Outcome Inflation?

While the Phase 3b results for centanafadine in ADHD + comorbid anxiety are statistically significant, the anxiety benefit was a secondary endpoint, not the primary trial aim — meaning it should be interpreted cautiously and is not part of the approved label. Independent researchers are calling for a dedicated Phase 3 trial in anxiety disorders before centanafadine's anti-anxiety properties are clinically promoted, noting that secondary-endpoint findings in ADHD trials have not always replicated.

Ibogaine Access in 2026: Executive Order Creates Momentum, but Safety Protocols Remain Inconsistent Across Centers

The April 2026 executive order aimed at accelerating treatments for serious mental illness has raised the profile of ibogaine, but most U.S. patients still travel to Mexico, Costa Rica, or Portugal for treatment. Reports from the field suggest wide variation in cardiac screening, medical supervision, and integration support across retreat centers — risks that peer-reviewed literature consistently flags. This space warrants close monitoring as regulatory interest grows and demand increases.
