This Week's Highlights

Magic Mushrooms Rewire the Brain in a Way Scientists Didn't Expect

A new Nature Neuroscience study found that psilocybin rapidly prompts brain cells to form and mature new synaptic connections — a faster structural change than researchers anticipated. The mechanism may partly explain psilocybin's antidepressant effects, though the work was done in rodents and authors caution human translation isn't yet established.

FDA-Cleared At-Home Brain Stimulation Shows One-in-Three Remission Rate for Severe Depression

The ProlivRx at-home transcranial magnetic stimulation device, approved by the FDA in January 2026 for adults with inadequate antidepressant response, showed remission in roughly one-third of patients with severe depression in the MOOD trial. Separately, a home tDCS device cleared in December 2025 is now commercially available in the U.S. as of September 2026, expanding non-pharmacological options outside the clinic.

APA November 2026 Journals: Veteran Mental Health, AI Trust, and Psilocybin Use Examined

The American Psychiatric Association's November 2026 journal issues cover a broad sweep of topics: prevalence of mental health conditions across three U.S. population cohorts, college students' trust (and distrust) of generative AI for mental health support, and clinical perspectives on therapeutic psilocybin use. The issue also features new research on home-based neuromodulation for depression.

Unpredictable Home Environments Independently Linked to Children's Mental Health Problems

A new study reported by PsyPost finds that household unpredictability — erratic schedules, inconsistent caregiving — predicts child mental health difficulties above and beyond abuse and neglect. The finding suggests that stability itself, not just safety, is a protective factor clinicians and social workers should assess.

EEG Neurometric Signature Identified for Depressive Symptom Severity

Researchers publishing in Nature Mental Health (November 5) described a phase-resetting rate modulation signal detectable via standard EEG that tracks depressive symptom severity in untreated adults. If validated in clinical settings, this biomarker could allow more objective treatment monitoring without expensive imaging.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Childhood Methylphenidate Treatment Associated With Lower Later Psychosis Risk

A landmark study published earlier this year in JAMA Psychiatry (University College Dublin / University of Edinburgh) followed nearly 4,000 young people with ADHD and found no evidence that methylphenidate raised psychosis risk — and early treatment was actually associated with lower long-term risk. Authors caution this needs replication, but the finding counters a longstanding concern about stimulant use in childhood.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) Real-World Data Show Cariprazine Improvements in Bipolar I Depression and MDD

AbbVie presented 12-week results from the CReW BP-I observational study at Psych Congress 2026, reporting improvements in depressive symptoms, daily functioning, and quality of life among 99 adults with bipolar I depression. The design is single-arm and uncontrolled, so drug-specific effects cannot be isolated — but results add to the growing real-world evidence base for cariprazine.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Effexor Supply Remains Intermittent in 2026; Japan Approves for Generalized Anxiety

Intermittent shortages of extended-release venlafaxine continue in the U.S., particularly for 37.5 mg and 225 mg strengths — patients are advised to check availability early and ask about equivalent generic alternatives. On the regulatory front, Japan's Ministry of Health approved Effexor SR for generalized anxiety disorder in March 2026, the first new national approval for the drug in several years.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

Multi-Lab Study Fails to Replicate Many Reported Psilocybin Effects in Mice

A coordinated five-laboratory replication study published November 2 found that many widely cited psilocybin effects in rodent models did not hold up — only some acute responses, such as suppressed fear, replicated reliably across sites. The findings don't negate human clinical data, but they raise important questions about which animal models are validating what, and reinforce the need for rigor in psychedelic preclinical research.

MDMA-Assisted Therapy: 67% of PTSD Patients No Longer Met Diagnostic Criteria in Clinical Trial

Live Science reported on findings from an MDMA-assisted therapy trial in which 67% of participants no longer met PTSD diagnostic criteria after treatment, compared with 32% in the placebo group. Though the FDA has not approved MDMA for any psychiatric condition, this trial data continues to influence policy discussions at the state and federal level.

Usona Institute Reportedly Files Psilocybin NDA for Major Depressive Disorder

Psychedelic Alpha reported November 2 that Usona Institute has completed its new drug application to the FDA for psilocybin in major depressive disorder. Usona has declined to confirm, and no FDA acknowledgment has been issued. If accepted, Usona's FDA priority voucher — granted in April — would compress the review window to 1–2 months. Compass Pathways' competing COMP360 rolling NDA for treatment-resistant depression is also underway.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

SSWR 2026: Scoping Review Maps Decolonizing Therapy Through Indigenous Epistemologies

A preliminary scoping review presented at the Society for Social Work and Research 2026 Annual Conference identified key components of decolonizing therapy, including storytelling, ritual, plant-based medicine, intergenerational healing of historical trauma, and community-based approaches. The review covered literature from 2020 to 2025 and frames Indigenous healing not as an add-on to Western psychiatry but as a distinct epistemological foundation.

APA-Indexed Paper Argues for Traditional Healing Frameworks Across Canada, China, Singapore, and the U.S.

A 2026 comparative paper indexed in PsycNet makes the case for integrating traditional healing systems into contemporary mental health practice, treating Western models as one framework among many rather than the universal standard. The authors call for increased research investment in how traditional healing works, so that methodological dominance of Western psychiatry is reduced and collaborative models can be developed on more equal footing.

Two-Eyed Seeing and Native-Led Telehealth Expanding Access on Reservations in 2026

A 2026 overview from Native Americans Today describes growing use of "Two-Eyed Seeing" — a Mi'kmaw-originated framework that holds Indigenous and Western knowledge simultaneously without forcing integration — and notes that Native-led telehealth platforms are meaningfully improving mental health access in rural and reservation communities where in-person care remains scarce.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Santa Clara County Opens $470 Million Behavioral Health Pavilion for Youth in Crisis

Santa Clara County opened its long-overdue youth psychiatric facility on November 3, 2026, providing 35 beds for children and teenagers in mental health crisis. The $470 million Behavioral Health Pavilion is the county's most significant behavioral health infrastructure investment in decades; a connected adult unit with 42 beds is also planned, part of a broader effort to add 530 treatment beds for residents with severe mental illness by 2030.

Ascension Recovery Services Opens Renewal Falls Behavioral Health Center in Cedar Falls, Iowa

Ascension Recovery Services announced the opening of Renewal Falls on September 10, 2026, a new residential addiction treatment and behavioral health facility in Cedar Falls, Iowa. The center offers medical detoxification, medication-assisted treatment, and integrated care for co-occurring mental health conditions.

Acadia Healthcare Opens Northern Virginia Adolescent Treatment Center

Acadia Healthcare celebrated the ribbon-cutting of its Northern Virginia Adolescent Treatment Center on March 19, 2026 — a new 16-bed residential facility in Chantilly, Virginia, serving adolescents with acute psychiatric and substance use needs. The facility expands Acadia's footprint in a region with a documented shortage of adolescent inpatient beds.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Usona Psilocybin NDA: Filed or Not? No Official Confirmation Yet

Psychedelic Alpha reported November 2 that Usona Institute has completed its psilocybin NDA for major depressive disorder — but Usona has declined to confirm and the FDA has not acknowledged receipt. If the filing is real and accepted, Usona's priority voucher would put a potential approval decision within months. Worth watching closely; check primary FDA and Usona sources before treating as settled.

UC Santa Cruz Team Claims Psilocybin's Benefits Can Be Separated From the Trip — in Mice

A University of California, Santa Cruz team reported boosting brain connectivity in mice using a psilocybin analog without producing trip-like behavioral effects. If a non-hallucinogenic version could deliver the same neuroplasticity benefits in humans, it would be a paradigm shift for psychedelic therapy — but this is animal research, and many similar claims have failed to translate. Worth watching as human data emerge.

Venlafaxine Supply Disruptions: Some Patients Switching Medications Mid-Treatment

Pharmacy-referral sites are reporting anecdotal accounts of patients being forced to switch antidepressants mid-treatment due to intermittent extended-release venlafaxine shortages. No FDA shortage alert has been issued, and independent verification is limited. Patients currently on Effexor XR who experience supply gaps should contact their prescriber before discontinuing, as abrupt stopping carries significant discontinuation syndrome risk.
