This Week's Highlights

APA September 2026 Journals: Circadian App for Mood Disorders, Pediatric Anxiety SMART Trial

The American Psychiatric Association's September journal releases spotlight two major trials: a multicenter, double-blind RCT testing a circadian rhythm stabilization app to prevent mood episode recurrence, and a pragmatic SMART study sequencing medication and CBT for pediatric anxiety. Together they represent a shift toward personalized, algorithm-guided care in both adult and child psychiatry.

Novel Orexin-2 Agonist ALKS 7290 Reduces ADHD Symptoms in 14 Days

Alkermes released Phase 1B proof-of-concept results showing ALKS 7290, a first-in-class orexin-2 receptor agonist, cut ADHD symptom severity scores by up to 19 points in just two weeks among 50 adults — with improvements appearing as early as day six. The drug targets the brain's wakefulness system rather than dopamine, opening a potential non-stimulant pathway for ADHD treatment.

By the Numbers: Global Mental Health Is Worsening

APA's September Monitor on Psychology reports that 8% of U.S. adults now see a mental health provider more than 10 times a year — double the 4% rate recorded in 2004 — and that Gen Z and millennials average more than four visits annually, more than twice the rate of baby boomers. Globally, conditions are deteriorating, driven primarily by the mental health toll of ongoing conflict and war.

Mental Health Research Trends to Watch in 2026

A September 20 roundup from Witan World identifies the dominant research themes reshaping psychiatry this year: psychedelic-assisted therapies entering Phase 3, AI-driven diagnostic tools, and the growing integration of social determinants of health into standard clinical workflows. The piece provides a useful framework for understanding how individual findings connect to broader field-wide shifts.

FDA Holds Public Hearing on Therapeutic Psychedelics This Month

The FDA's long-anticipated public hearing on the potential therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs — announced in the Federal Register in July — took place in September, marking the most significant federal regulatory engagement with psychedelic medicine in fifty years. The hearing follows a wave of executive orders, expedited review vouchers, and interagency agreements issued between April and July 2026.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Methylphenidate Shortages Persist as Non-Stimulant Simtriyo Fills Gap

Extended-release methylphenidate formulations — including generic Concerta and Ritalin LA in 18 mg, 27 mg, 36 mg, and 54 mg doses — remain on the FDA shortage list with intermittent availability. In parallel, the FDA approved Simtriyo (centanafadine), a first-of-its-kind triple reuptake inhibitor for ages six and up in July 2026, giving prescribers a non-stimulant alternative amid ongoing supply constraints.

Vraylar (cariprazine) AbbVie Presents Real-World Vraylar Data at Psych Congress 2026

At Psych Congress in New Orleans (September 15–19), AbbVie presented prospective real-world data showing cariprazine produced meaningful improvements in depressive symptoms, daily functioning, and quality of life in adults with MDD and bipolar I depression — confirming that clinical trial benefits hold up in routine practice with more complex patients. The data reinforce cariprazine's use as an adjunct antidepressant following its December 2025 expanded pediatric approval.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Two-Decade FAERS Review Flags New Post-Marketing Safety Signals for Venlafaxine

A 2026 study published in Frontiers in Pharmacology analyzed 47,325 venlafaxine-related adverse event reports from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System spanning two decades, identifying emerging safety signals that may not have been apparent in pre-approval trials. The findings are relevant given that venlafaxine ranked 51st among all U.S. prescriptions in 2023, with over 13.2 million scripts dispensed annually.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

Ibogaine Enters Formal Clinical Trials in 12 U.S. States

As of 2026, ibogaine clinical trials are underway in twelve states, with modified compounds designed to deliver neuroplasticity benefits while eliminating the QTc cardiac prolongation that caused rare deaths with earlier formulations. A new PMC study on magnesium ibogaine reports rapid subjective recovery from neuropsychiatric disorders, though researchers caution the data remain preliminary and cardiac monitoring protocols are essential.

Hopkins Psilocybin Research: Phase 3 Trials Underway for Depression and MDD

Johns Hopkins Psychedelics Research continues to advance psilocybin toward mainstream approval, with multiple Phase 3 trials demonstrating significant effects for treatment-resistant depression and major depressive disorder. The FDA has granted Breakthrough Therapy designation to psilocybin for both TRD and MDD, and the University of North Carolina expects completion of a psilocybin study this month, with results expected to strengthen the regulatory case for approval.

Breathwork and Somatic Therapy Go Mainstream as Clinical Evidence Grows

Somatic therapies — including guided breathwork, grounding techniques, and movement-based nervous system regulation — are moving from complementary to primary in trauma-informed care settings. A 2026 meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found meaningful reductions in stress biomarkers from structured breathwork, particularly for individuals with anxiety disorders, PTSD, and histories of complex trauma.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

Psychiatric Times: Beyond Compliance — What's Still Missing in Indigenous Mental Health Care

Psychiatric Times examines how dominant clinical models continue to pathologize Indigenous behaviors and beliefs while ignoring systemic oppression and intergenerational trauma. The piece argues that true decolonizing care requires dismantling the individual-lens assumptions baked into standard psychiatric diagnosis — interpreting distress through family, kinship, and community rather than isolated symptom clusters — and that institutional barriers (licensing constraints, funding gaps) remain the primary obstacle to genuine change.

APA Chapter: Decolonizing Mental Health Practice Through Traditional Healing

A newly published APA chapter outlines how culturally grounded care — integrating storytelling, ritual, plant-based medicine, and community-based interventions — produces outcomes that Western diagnostic frameworks are not designed to measure. The authors argue that prioritizing collective well-being over individual pathology and actively centering Indigenous epistemologies is not a cultural accommodation but a clinical imperative backed by outcomes data from communities where these approaches are practiced.

SSWR 2026 Conference: Centering Ancestral Healing in Mental Health Practice

The Society for Social Work and Research's 30th Annual Conference featured a presentation titled "Decolonizing Therapy: Centering Indigenous and Ancestral Healing in Mental Health Practices," emphasizing how ancestral healing traditions — including spiritual practices, land-based healing, and intergenerational knowledge transmission — provide resilience frameworks that clinical therapy alone cannot replicate. The presentation called for licensing and funding structures that formally recognize and support these modalities.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

California Governor Announces 15 New Mental Health Projects, Including Yurok Tribe Treatment Center

Governor Newsom's August 2026 mental health expansion round includes 15 projects statewide, with notable highlights including two 16-bed Mental Health Recovery Centers on the Metropolitan State Hospital campus (~$65M) and the Yurok Tribe's Wah-Sekw-Won Treatment Center in Humboldt County (~$26M), which will provide 53 residential beds for adults and integrate Indigenous healing alongside clinical behavioral health services.

New Mexico Adds Five Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics in 2026

New Mexico's Health Care Authority is expanding its network of Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHCs) by five new agencies across six counties, providing 24/7 access to integrated mental health and substance use disorder services regardless of insurance status. CCBHCs are designed to be the safety-net entry point for the most underserved populations, offering primary care screening alongside psychiatric care.

Penn State Health Moves Psychiatric Services to Holy Spirit Medical Center

Penn State Health completed its transition of inpatient psychiatric services from the Pennsylvania Psychiatric Institute to Holy Spirit Medical Center, rebranding the service line as Penn State Health Psychiatry and Behavioral Health. The move integrates psychiatric care more tightly with acute general hospital services, an evidence-based model that improves outcomes for patients with co-occurring medical and psychiatric conditions.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Orexin-2 Agonist ALKS 7290: Promising Phase 1B ADHD Data, But Very Early

The ALKS 7290 results making headlines involved only 50 adults over 14 days of inpatient treatment — an extremely small, short, and controlled setting. While the AISRS score reductions are notable and the orexin mechanism is theoretically sound, no long-term safety, functional outcome, or real-world data yet exist. Worth tracking closely as Phase 2 recruitment begins, but the excitement is running well ahead of the evidence.

Magnesium Ibogaine for Rapid Neuropsychiatric Recovery: Preliminary Case Reports

A PMC preprint describes anecdotal and case-report-level accounts of magnesium ibogaine producing rapid subjective recovery from neuropsychiatric symptoms including depression and PTSD. The magnesium formulation is proposed to reduce cardiac risk while preserving the neuroplasticity benefits, but this work is case-series level — no RCT data exist yet, and cardiac safety concerns with ibogaine derivatives remain unresolved at scale. Credible enough to follow; far too early to act on.

Centanafadine (Simtriyo) FDA Approval — Long-Term Efficacy and Abuse Potential Data Pending

Simtriyo (centanafadine) received FDA approval in July 2026 as the first triple reuptake inhibitor (dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin) for ADHD across ages six and up, with a non-stimulant scheduling claim. However, post-market studies on long-term efficacy beyond clinical trial conditions and real-world abuse potential data are not yet available. Early prescriber experience will be important to monitor over the coming 12–18 months.
