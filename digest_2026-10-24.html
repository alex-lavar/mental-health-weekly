This Week's Highlights

FDA-Cleared Brain Mapping Treatment Reports 68% Remission Rate for PTSD

A newly FDA-cleared neuromodulation therapy uses personalized brain mapping combined with targeted magnetic stimulation to identify and treat abnormal neural activity linked to PTSD. A clinical trial reported a 52% reduction in symptoms and a 68% remission rate — figures that, if they hold under broader scrutiny, would mark a significant advance over existing PTSD therapies. The approach is individualized to each patient's brain profile, which researchers say may explain its elevated efficacy compared to standard TMS.

Yale Scientists: Cortisol Helps the Brain Lock In Emotional Memories

Published October 16, a Yale study offers new insight into why stressful events are often remembered so vividly. Researchers found that the stress hormone cortisol actively assists in converting emotionally charged experiences into stronger, longer-lasting memories — a mechanism that may illuminate why trauma memories are so persistent. The finding has potential implications for understanding PTSD and anxiety disorders, where intrusive memory is a core symptom.

Depression May Be Five Different Disorders, Brain-Activity Study Finds

A study of 263 people diagnosed with depression identified five distinct patterns of brain activity, raising the possibility that what clinicians treat as a single condition is in fact a cluster of biologically different illnesses. If replicated, this finding could help explain why antidepressants work for some patients and not others, and may eventually guide more targeted prescribing. Researchers caution that larger studies are needed before this framework reaches clinical practice.

Loneliness Robs Men of Nearly Six Years Free from Depression and Anxiety

A large population study found that social isolation and loneliness significantly shorten the period of life spent free from depression and anxiety. Men who experienced both lost approximately 5.8 healthy years, while women lost roughly 3.7. The finding adds to a growing body of evidence that social connectedness is a primary determinant of mental health — not merely a correlate — and underscores calls to treat loneliness as a public health priority.

Massive Study Links Teen Marijuana Use to Double the Risk of Serious Mental Illness

A large-scale epidemiological study reported that adolescents who use cannabis regularly are at roughly twice the risk of developing a serious mental illness compared to non-users. Researchers acknowledge the challenge of disentangling causation from correlation — teens with pre-existing vulnerabilities may also be more likely to use cannabis — but say the magnitude of the association warrants stronger prevention messaging. The findings come as legal cannabis markets continue to expand in several U.S. states and countries.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Extended-Release Methylphenidate Shortage Continues Into Late 2026

Extended-release formulations of methylphenidate — including generic Concerta and Ritalin LA — remain on national shortage lists in 2026, while immediate-release versions are generally available. The shortage is driving some patients and prescribers toward newer alternatives. In July 2026, the FDA approved centanafadine (brand name Simtriyo), the first triple reuptake inhibitor approved for ADHD, providing a non-stimulant option that some providers may now consider for patients who can't obtain methylphenidate.

Vraylar (cariprazine) AbbVie Presents Real-World Vraylar Data at Psych Congress 2026: PHQ-9 Scores Cut Nearly in Half

AbbVie presented two observational studies at Psych Congress 2026 covering cariprazine's use in major depressive disorder and bipolar I disorder. In an interim analysis of 76 participants, mean PHQ-9 depression scores dropped from 15.7 at baseline to 7.1 at week 6 — a near-halving of measured symptom burden. A separate Psychiatric Times analysis noted that cariprazine adjunctive to antidepressant therapy also reduced anhedonia subscale scores, which remain a difficult-to-treat dimension of depression.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) New OCD Guidance: Venlafaxine Not Recommended as First-Line Treatment

An August 2026 psychopharmacology guidance update clarified that venlafaxine (Effexor), an SNRI widely used for depression and anxiety, is not recommended as a first-line treatment for obsessive-compulsive disorder. The update reviewed evidence and placed it behind SSRIs in the OCD treatment hierarchy. For patients already using venlafaxine for co-occurring conditions, clinicians are advised to consult updated guidelines when also addressing OCD symptoms.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

Compass Pathways Targets Q4 2026 for Psilocybin New Drug Application

Compass Pathways, whose COMP360 psilocybin formulation has been in late-stage trials for treatment-resistant depression, is targeting Q4 2026 for submission of its New Drug Application to the FDA. A public comment period on related FDA guidance closed October 5. Regulatory approval remains uncertain, but the NDA submission — if completed on schedule — would move psilocybin closer to legal prescription use in the United States than it has ever been.

Executive Order Directs At Least $50 Million Toward Psychedelic Therapy Research

A Penn LDI research update covers an executive order that has given structured momentum to psychedelic-assisted therapy development. The order reportedly allocates at least $50 million toward psychedelic research for serious mental health conditions. The FDA also announced that three psychedelic-focused companies would receive priority review vouchers — two for psilocybin-based treatments for severe depression and one for an MDMA metabolite targeting PTSD. Researchers note that vouchers are discretionary, not approval guarantees.

Psilocybin Plus Therapy Linked to Reduced Cocaine Use in Human Trial; Alcohol Research Also Advances

A human clinical trial this week linked psilocybin-assisted therapy to meaningful reductions in cocaine use, adding to a growing body of evidence for psychedelics in treating substance use disorders. Separately, preliminary research pointed to a possible role for psilocybin in alcohol treatment. A preclinical study also suggested the compound may protect sensory nerves from chemotherapy-induced damage, opening a potential line of investigation beyond psychiatric indications. All findings are early-stage and require larger replication trials.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

SSWR 2026 Scoping Review Identifies Core Features of Decolonizing Therapy Across Global Literature

A scoping review presented at the 2026 Society for Social Work and Research conference analyzed published literature from 2020–2025 on decolonizing therapy. Researchers identified five recurring features: incorporating Indigenous epistemologies, addressing historical trauma, using community-based approaches, centering ancestral healing practices, and challenging the individualistic assumptions of Western psychotherapy models. The review also identified significant research gaps, noting that empirical outcome studies on these approaches remain scarce compared to qualitative and descriptive work.

Psychiatric Times: Indigenous Mental Health Requires Reimagining, Not Just Cultural Add-Ons

A Psychiatric Times clinical commentary argues that the mental health system's current approach to Indigenous populations — primarily adding culturally sensitive elements to unchanged Western frameworks — falls short of what is needed. The authors contend that practices such as talking circles, smudging, drumming, and storytelling are not supplements to be grafted onto clinical care, but reflect fundamentally different epistemologies of healing that merit their own evidence base and institutional legitimacy. Many Indigenous veterans, the piece notes, prefer traditional ceremonies like sweat lodges over conventional PTSD therapy.

New Paper Calls for Non-Western Perspectives in Counselling: Lessons from India's Healing Traditions

A 2026 paper in a peer-reviewed counselling journal examines how Indian and other non-Western knowledge systems can reshape global mental health practice. The authors challenge psychotherapy's individualistic focus and advocate for a holistic approach that integrates spiritual, communal, and ancestral elements — drawing from Ayurvedic medicine, yogic philosophy, and local healing traditions. The piece contributes to a growing international effort to decolonize counselling curricula and widen what counts as therapeutic evidence.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Michigan Dedicates New Psychiatric Hospital — the State's First to Serve Children

A new state psychiatric hospital in Northville Township, Michigan, was officially dedicated on October 7 and is set to open to patients later this month. Critically, it will be the only psychiatric hospital in Michigan designed to serve children, addressing a long-standing gap in the state's inpatient mental health capacity. Michigan has faced a documented shortage of psychiatric beds for pediatric patients, and advocates say the facility represents a meaningful step toward closing that gap.

Valley Health Opens New Behavioral Health Pavilion in Winchester, Virginia — October 26

Valley Health's new Behavioral Health Pavilion officially opens October 26 in Winchester, Virginia. The facility includes a digital health center with integrated telehealth capabilities, designed to extend reach to patients in surrounding rural areas. The opening adds dedicated psychiatric space to a regional health system that previously relied on dispersed outpatient services, and represents part of a broader trend of health systems investing in standalone behavioral health infrastructure.

Arkview Behavioral Health Opens Purpose-Built Residential Facility in Fayetteville, Pennsylvania

Arkview Behavioral Health announced on October 12 that it has opened a residential facility in Fayetteville, PA, dedicated entirely to primary mental health treatment — not addiction — serving people with depression, anxiety, trauma, and mood disorders who require more support than outpatient care can provide. The facility is notable for its focus on mental health as a standalone specialty in a residential setting, a model that remains relatively rare compared to dual-diagnosis and addiction-focused residential programs.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Ibogaine Clinic Claims 88% PTSD Symptom Reduction — No Published Clinical Trial Behind the Figure

A private ibogaine treatment provider is circulating a claim of 88% average reduction in PTSD symptoms at one month post-treatment. Ibogaine, derived from the African iboga plant, has shown promise in early investigational work, and federal interest in funding psychedelic research has grown — but ibogaine remains unapproved in the United States, and no controlled clinical trial has been published to support this specific figure. The compound also carries documented cardiac risks. Worth tracking as research matures, but the headline number requires peer-reviewed backing before clinical interpretation.

Psilocybin May Protect Nerve Cells From Chemotherapy Damage — Preclinical Research Only

A preclinical study raised the possibility that psilocybin may help protect sensory neurons from chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, a painful and often persistent side effect of cancer treatment. The research is early-stage animal work, and the pathway from preclinical findings to human trials is long and frequently unsuccessful — but it marks an intriguing potential application of psychedelics beyond psychiatric indications. Researchers have called for further investigation before any clinical implications are drawn.

Small Pilot Study: Eliminating Ultra-Processed Foods Improves Depression Symptoms

A small preliminary study found that people with depression who sharply reduced their consumption of ultra-processed foods experienced notable improvements in depressive symptoms. The sample size is too small for firm conclusions, and the study design cannot isolate the dietary change from confounding lifestyle factors — but the finding aligns with a growing literature linking gut microbiome health, inflammation, and mood disorders. Larger randomized trials are needed to determine whether dietary intervention belongs in mainstream depression treatment.
