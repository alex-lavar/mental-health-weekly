This Week's Highlights

U.S. to Invest Millions in Psychedelics Research on Ibogaine, Psilocybin, and Ketamine

The Department of Health and Human Services announced up to $4.9 million in new research funding to advance psychedelic-assisted treatments for mental health conditions, including PTSD. The push follows an April executive order and places ibogaine — rarely studied in the U.S. — alongside psilocybin and ketamine as federal research priorities for the first time.

Magic Mushrooms Rewire the Brain in a Way Scientists Didn't Expect

A University of California, Santa Cruz study published in Nature Neuroscience found that psilocybin's structural remodeling of brain cell connections can be separated from the pathways responsible for the subjective psychedelic experience. The finding raises important questions about whether the "trip" is actually necessary for therapeutic benefit — a debate with major clinical implications.

Compass Pathways Files Psilocybin NDA for Treatment-Resistant Depression

Compass Pathways submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA on November 9 for its psilocybin therapy targeting treatment-resistant depression. With a competing application from Usona Institute also reportedly filed, the FDA could rule on the first approvable psychedelic medicine within months — marking a potential turning point for the field.

JAMA Psychiatry: Stepped-Care Model Proposed for Student Mental Health in Digital Age

A special communication published November 11 in JAMA Psychiatry calls for a structured stepped-care approach to campus mental health that integrates digital interventions — apps, AI tools, and telehealth — alongside traditional counseling. The authors argue that without clear triage protocols, digital tools risk displacing rather than supplementing evidence-based care.

New Research Ties Extreme Weather to Mental Health Challenges in Africa

A study led by Professor Collins Iwuji of the Africa Health Research Institute, released ahead of COP31, found significantly higher rates of mental disorders among people exposed to flooding across four African countries. The research adds to a growing evidence base linking climate disruption to psychological harm, particularly in regions with the least mental health infrastructure.

Medication Watch

Methylphenidate Methylphenidate Linked to GI Symptoms and Appetite Suppression in New Review

A PLOS One review covered this week found that children and teens taking methylphenidate have higher odds of decreased appetite and gastrointestinal symptoms compared to untreated peers. While these effects are well-known, the review quantifies their prevalence and calls for more routine GI monitoring in pediatric ADHD treatment protocols.

Methylphenidate Weekend "Drug Holidays" Show No Overall Benefit for Adults With ADHD

An observational study in Psychiatry Research tracked 50 adult men with ADHD over 28 days and found that planned weekend breaks from methylphenidate offered no clear overall advantage over daily use. Those on drug holidays reported higher procrastination and lower task completion — suggesting that the common practice of skipping weekend doses warrants reconsideration in adults.

Vraylar (Cariprazine) AbbVie Presents Real-World Vraylar Data at Psych Congress 2026

AbbVie's CReW BP-I study of 118 adults with bipolar I depression showed mean MADRS scores dropping from 32.2 at baseline to 19.9 at week 12 on Vraylar. Interim data from the ProACt MDD study also showed PHQ-9 scores falling from 15.7 to 7.1 by week 6. These are company-sponsored observational results, not controlled trials, and should be interpreted accordingly.

Venlafaxine (Effexor) Viatris Reports Positive Phase 3 Results for Effexor in Japanese Adults With GAD

Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare approved Effexor SR for generalized anxiety disorder following a Phase 3 trial that met its primary endpoint at 8 weeks. The approval expands venlafaxine's reach in Asia, though the trial reported a 7.3% discontinuation rate due to treatment-emergent adverse events, compared to 1.7% for placebo.

Alternative & Plant Medicine

U.S. Offers Up to $4.9 Million for First-Ever Ibogaine PTSD Studies

Federal officials announced funding to lay the groundwork for what could become the first American clinical trials of ibogaine for PTSD. The drug — derived from an African shrub and long used in West African Bwiti ceremonies — has shown dramatic results in small international studies, but its cardiac risks have kept it Schedule I in the U.S. This funding represents a meaningful shift in federal posture toward the substance.

Psilocybin's Healing Mechanism May Not Require the Psychedelic Experience Itself

The UC Santa Cruz Nature Neuroscience study found that the cellular pathway responsible for psilocybin's structural brain changes can be activated independently of the serotonin 2A receptor pathway that drives the subjective psychedelic "trip." If replicated, this could lead to non-hallucinogenic psilocybin analogs that deliver therapeutic neuroplasticity without the experiential demands of a guided psychedelic session.

APA November Journals: Psilocybin Use Patterns and Home Neuromodulation for Depression

The November 2026 issues of The American Journal of Psychiatry and affiliated journals include a population study on past-year psilocybin use in the U.S. and research on home-based neuromodulation as a scalable treatment for depression. Together, the articles signal growing mainstream psychiatric interest in approaches once considered fringe or investigational.

Indigenous Healing — History & Practice

SSWR 2026: Scoping Review Finds Indigenous Epistemologies Underrepresented in Formal Mental Health Frameworks

A Society for Social Work and Research conference abstract presents preliminary findings from a scoping review of 2020–2025 literature on decolonizing therapy. Researchers found that while Indigenous practices like storytelling, ritual, and plant medicine are increasingly cited, they remain peripheral in formal clinical guidelines — with most integration happening in community and advocacy settings rather than regulated healthcare.

New APA-Published Chapter Maps Decolonial Mental Health Practice Through Traditional Healing

A 2026 chapter published through APA's PsycNET examines how Indigenous healing systems — including ceremony, land-based practices, and elder guidance — can inform decolonial mental health care. The authors argue that Western psychiatry's pathology-first framework has historically failed Indigenous communities and that culturally grounded healing must be treated as clinically legitimate, not supplementary.

The Ancestral Institute's Model for Decolonial Community Psychology Gains Academic Recognition

A February 2026 article in the Journal of Community and Applied Psychology presents the Ancestral Institute's approach to combining ancestral healing with contemporary psychology for Indigenous, Black, and other communities of color. The model centers community-led design and historical trauma as a clinical baseline rather than an add-on, and is drawing increasing attention in graduate training programs.

Global Treatment Centers & Announcements

Thresholds Opens 24/7 Free Crisis Center on Chicago's South Side

Chicago nonprofit Thresholds launched "The Living Room," a free, nonclinical 24/7 crisis stabilization center in the Canaryville neighborhood on November 13. The model — walk-in, peer-staffed, no insurance required — is designed to divert people from emergency rooms during mental health crises. The South Side location addresses a longstanding gap in community mental health access in one of Chicago's most underserved areas.

Arkview Behavioral Health Opens New Inpatient Facility in Fayetteville, Pennsylvania

Arkview Behavioral Health opened its newest facility in Fayetteville, PA on November 9, offering exclusively behavioral health treatment separate from substance use services. The facility is Arkview's fourth in the state and focuses on a less-siloed clinical model, with integrated care teams addressing mood disorders, trauma, and psychosis under one roof.

New 32-Bed Inpatient Mental Health Facility Set to Open in Kennewick, Washington

Aristo Healthcare's New Hopes Kennewick is slated to open November 17 at 7319 W. Hood Place in Kennewick, WA. The 32-bed inpatient facility aims to address a critical shortage of psychiatric beds in Eastern Washington, a region where patients have historically faced multi-week waits for inpatient placement.

Oklahoma Children's OU Health Celebrates Opening of New Pediatric Behavioral Health Center

State leaders gathered in Oklahoma City on November 5 to mark the grand opening of the Oklahoma Children's OU Health Behavioral Health Center, which will create more than 400 new clinical and non-clinical jobs. The center represents a major expansion of pediatric psychiatric capacity in a state that has chronically ranked among the lowest in the U.S. for mental health access.

Universal Health Services Closes Third Behavioral Health Facility of 2026

Talbott Recovery, a UHS-owned outpatient addiction treatment and mental health clinic in Atlanta, shuttered earlier this month — the third UHS-affiliated site to close in 2026. The pattern raises questions about the financial sustainability of outpatient behavioral health under current reimbursement models, even within large hospital systems.

Unverified / Worth Watching

Usona Institute Reportedly Files Psilocybin NDA for Major Depressive Disorder — Unconfirmed

Psychedelic Alpha reports that nonprofit Usona Institute has filed a New Drug Application for psilocybin to treat major depressive disorder, potentially creating a two-NDA race with Compass Pathways. However, Usona declined to comment and the FDA has not confirmed receipt. If verified, this would make MDD — not just treatment-resistant depression — an FDA review target for psilocybin. Worth watching for official confirmation.

AI Therapy Chatbots Gaining Ground With Limited Clinical Validation

As AI-powered mental health apps proliferate, researchers are sounding alarms: a review found that fewer than 22% of top mental health apps cite actual scientific studies to support their marketing claims. General-purpose large language models are increasingly being used in therapeutic-adjacent roles they were not designed or validated for. Short-term mood improvements have been reported in some trials, but long-term efficacy, safety guardrails, and professional oversight standards remain unestablished. Promising, but proceed carefully.
